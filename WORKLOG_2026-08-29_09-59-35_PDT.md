## Design decisions

### Compose canonical content instead of duplicating it

The map model is static and typed, but it is produced from topic collection records. Only map-specific labels and coordinates were added to topic frontmatter. This prevents topic pages and the SVG from drifting apart as names, descriptions, maturity, or relationships change.

### Keep geometry editable but renderer-neutral

Positions describe semantic node centers, hit-area dimensions, and small label offsets on a stable canvas. They do not contain SVG paths, stroke details, DOM state, or interaction flags. Step 11 remains free to draw irregular curves without changing the content contract.

### Model hierarchy as data

The central LLM node, major area nodes, and topic nodes are connected with `contains` edges. The future renderer will not need hard-coded knowledge about which category belongs to which branch.

### Derive semantic edges from topic relationships

Progression edges come from `leadsTo`, prerequisite edges come from prerequisites not already represented by the same progression, and related edges are normalized into one undirected pair. This avoids duplicate strokes while preserving relationship meaning.

### Require V1 geometry for mapped topics

Every visible topic passed to the V1 composer must have an in-canvas position and an area layout. Adding a new topic page remains lightweight, but deliberately placing it on the public map is an explicit editorial step.

### Keep future modes deliberately unspecified

The empty `extensions` record creates a compatible place for future features without prematurely fixing learning-path, overlay, or frontier-mode schemas before their interaction designs exist.
