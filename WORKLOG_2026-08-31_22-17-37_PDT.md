## Scaling assessment

1. **Dozens of articles are safe.** Astro creates one static route per published article, the homepage already limits itself to five recent articles, and Pagefind is designed for much larger static sites.
2. **Tests need to become data-driven before article three.** Exact fixture counts in writing, discovery, content-preview, and production checks would fail even when the new article is valid.
3. **The archive needs pagination later.** It renders every article into one HTML page and filters every DOM entry on each change. This is reasonable for dozens but should be paginated around 75–100 entries.
4. **Topic backlinks need a display limit later.** A popular topic currently lists every associated article; show recent entries plus an archive link once those lists become long.
5. **RSS should eventually be capped.** It currently includes every published article; a latest-20 or latest-50 feed will remain compact.
6. **Manual article sequencing is optional but brittle at scale.** `previous` and `next` can be omitted; if chronological navigation becomes a core feature, derive it rather than maintaining reciprocal IDs manually.
7. **Hosting is not the near-term limit.** GitHub Pages currently allows a published site up to 1 GB and a deployment up to 10 minutes. Build-time measurement should warn well before those limits.

## Decision

Section 2 can proceed without redesigning article storage. Before publishing a third article, make the regression suite discover published entries dynamically. Add pagination, bounded topic lists, and an RSS cap only when the archive approaches the measured threshold rather than prematurely.
